# AI in Hiring: US Law Reference (Federal + 50 States + DC) — v4

Prepared for Michael Indelicato, Aggregate Trades LLC (commercial GC; HQ Chesapeake, VA; offices in Milton, GA and the Knoxville, TN area; works in the 48 contiguous states) · Status as of **2026-10-29** · Supersedes `ai-hiring-laws-by-state-2026-10-29-v3.md` (see changelog at bottom)

**How to read this:** "Yes" in the state table means an enacted law or binding rule whose text *specifically* covers AI or automated tools used in hiring or employment decisions. Pending bills, failed bills, vetoes, public-sector-only rules, and general AI or consumer laws are marked "No", with a note. General anti-discrimination law applies to AI tools in **every** state, the same way it applies to any other hiring method.

**Test applied to borderline rows (TX, NJ):** a row is "Yes" only if the binding text itself names AI/automated tools *in an employment context*. A general AI law that only reaches employment by covering "any" use is "No (general AI law)".

**Status values:** enacted (law passed, not yet operative) · effective (in force) · pending (bill alive or last action shown without final disposition) · none found (as of the date, by the method stated).

## 1. Federal

No federal statute is written specifically for AI hiring tools. The existing laws apply to any selection tool, AI or not.

## 3. What Applies to Michael

### Home states: Virginia (primary), Georgia and Tennessee (office states)
Every hire follows the law of the state (and locality) where the job is. For jobs in any other state, use that state's row in §2.

#### Virginia (primary state)
- **No Virginia statute specifically regulates private-employer AI in hiring** (none found as of 2026-10-29; see VA row). HB 2094 was vetoed and the veto sustained; the 2026 employment-AI bills were left in committee or continued to 2027. The VCDPA profiling opt-out excludes applicant and employee data (§59.1-576(C)(14)). [DS VA/GA 10-29]
- **Virginia Human Rights Act** applies to AI-assisted decisions the same as human ones. **Threshold: 5 or more employees.** §2.2-3905(A): "'Employer' means a person employing (i) five or more employees for each working day in each of 20 or more calendar weeks in the current or preceding calendar year, and any agent of such a person, or (ii) one or more domestic workers." Protected classes are in §2.2-3905(B)(1)(a); "age" means 40 or older. [§2.2-3905](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/2.2-3905/) [DS VA/GA 10-29]
- **Cross-reference (not an AI rule), E-Verify:** §40.1-11.2 covers **state agencies only**. §2.2-4308.2(B) covers employers with "more than an average of 50 employees for the previous 12 months" entering a state-agency contract "in excess of $50,000"; under (C), noncompliance means debarment from state-agency contracting "for a period up to one year". §2.2-4317(E) covers prequalification denial. #### All home states
- **Federal law still applies** at its own thresholds: Title VII and ADA (15+), ADEA (20+) (federal thresholds per statute, not rechecked here), the Uniform Guidelines four-fifths rule for any screening tool, and FCRA if a third party supplies background checks or candidate scores. EO 14281 tells federal agencies to deprioritize disparate-impact enforcement but does not change the statute or stop private suits.
- **Practical minimums for any AI or ATS screening:** know what the tool screens on; get the vendor's adverse-impact or bias-testing data in writing; keep a human decision-maker; offer an accommodation or alternative path (timed or video assessments); keep applicant-flow records.

### When hiring or recruiting remote or out-of-state candidates
The law of the **candidate's or job's location** generally controls. Triggers:
- **NYC:** AEDT used for an NYC job needs a bias audit within one year before use, public audit information, and notice 10 business days before use. *(Remote-role scope is in the DCWP FAQ; not re-read.)*
- **Illinois:** notice to employees of AI use in covered decisions and no zip-code proxies (IHRA 2-102(L)); applicant-notice details await IDHR rules. AI-analyzed video interviews for Illinois-based positions: notice, explanation, consent; delete within 30 days on request.
- **California:** CRD ADS regs (records 4 years; ADS can violate FEHA). *(FEHA's 5-employee threshold was not re-read.)* CPPA ADMT duties by 1/1/2027 only if you're a CCPA "business" ($26.625M+ revenue, or 100k+ CA consumers/households, or 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing PI). From 7/1/2027 (SB 947): no sole reliance on ADS for discipline or termination, plus post-use notice. AB 1883: no AI emotion-inference or neural-data surveillance tools.
- **Colorado (from 1/1/2027):** ADMT duties for deployers doing business in CO; no employee-count exemption found in the signed act. Enforcement is stayed under the *xAI v. Weiser* stipulation until 14 days after a PI ruling. Final AG rules pending.
- **Maryland:** no facial-recognition templates in interviews without a signed waiver.
- **New Jersey:** LAD disparate-impact rules expressly reach AEDTs; test tools for adverse impact before use and give applicants a way to request accommodation.
- **Connecticut:** AI use is no defense under CFEPA from 10/1/2026; pre-decision written notice for tools deployed on/after 10/1/2027.
- **Texas (not counted as Yes):** only intentional AI discrimination is barred; AG enforces with a 60-day cure; local AI ordinances preempted.
- **Simplest compliant approach for a multi-state pool:** pre-use notice of AI; consent before any AI video or face analysis; annual independent bias audit if you use automated scoring/ranking; human review and an appeal path; an accommodation route; 4-year record retention.
