# 01 — Interview Method and Scoring (required every interview)

This method follows four published systems:
- the U.S. OPM *Structured Interview Guide*
- SHRM guidance on behavioral interviews and anchored rating scales
- Google re:Work's structured-interviewing guide
- the 2022 Sackett et al. meta-analysis, which found structured interviews were the strongest single predictor of job performance (operational validity about .42), ahead of job-knowledge tests (about .40) and work samples (about .33)

The kit pairs a structured interview with a work-sample or knowledge check for each role. Sources are listed in `sources.md`.

## 1. Question types
- **Behavioral (B):** "Tell me about a time you…" Past behavior, scored on specifics: situation, action, result.
- **Situational (S):** "What would you do if…" A realistic job scenario, scored on approach.
- **Technical / knowledge (T):** A trade or system fact the role must know, scored on accuracy.
- **Work sample (W):** Do or describe a real task, such as the estimator test, reading a layout, or a tool walk-through.

Standard probes for every B and S question:
- "What exactly did *you* do?"
- "What tool or system did you use?"
- "How did you check it was right?"
- "What was the result, and the number if you have one?"
- "What would you do differently?"

## 2. Opening script (read the same way to every candidate)
> "Thanks for coming in. We use the same set of questions for everyone applying for this position so it's fair and consistent. I'll take notes. Some questions are about your trade skills, and some are about the tools, apps, and software you use and how they've changed your work. There are no trick questions. If you don't know something, just say so; that's a fine answer. Do you need anything to make this interview work for you?"

## 3. Rating scale (1–5, OPM-style anchors)
| Score | Label | General meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Expert | Handles the hardest version of the situation. Teaches others. Specific, verified examples with measurable results. Names the tools *and* their limits. |
| 4 | Advanced | Handles hard situations with little guidance. Gives specific examples. Checks their own work. |
| 3 | Intermediate (meets the bar for mid-level) | Handles normal job situations and needs occasional guidance. The example is real but light on results. |
| 2 | Basic | Familiar with the topic and needs frequent guidance. Vague or second-hand example. |
| 1 | Awareness | Little or no relevant experience, an incorrect answer, or no example. |
| NS | Not scored | Notes are insufficient. Don't guess. |

Each answer key gives question-specific **Go** (score 4–5) and **No-Go** (score 1–2) indicators. A score of 3 is "meets": it has the core of the Go answer without the depth.

## 4. Decision rules
1. **Knockouts first.** Any knockout listed in the role's answer key means **No-Go**, whatever the scores.
2. **Average** of all scored questions, with equal weights. If weights differ, the tuning block must document why (OPM recommends equal weights unless a reason is documented).
3. **Thresholds:**

   | Seniority | Go if average ≥ | and every ★ question ≥ | Hold band |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Entry / apprentice | 2.5 | 2 | 2.2–2.49 |
   | Mid / journeyman | 3.0 | 3 | 2.7–2.99 |
   | Senior / lead / foreman | 3.5 | 3 | 3.2–3.49 |

4. **Hold** means get more information before deciding:
   - a reference check focused on the weak competency
   - a paid skills demo or a second interview
   - verification of a claimed certification

   Turn the Hold into Go or No-Go within 5 business days.
5. **The work-sample test** (where used) counts as one ★ question, scaled to 1–5 with the formula in the test's answer key.
6. **Certifications:** a required certification that can't be verified means Hold until it's verified, or No-Go if it's a legal requirement for the work (for example, EPA 608 for handling refrigerant).

## 5. Interviewer conduct
- Take notes of what was said, not impressions. Score right after the interview, independently, before talking it over with others (Google re:Work and OPM practice).
- With more than one interviewer, compare scores. Discuss any gap of 2 or more points and record the agreed score and the reason.
- Use the same interviewer(s) for all candidates for a position where possible.
- Keep notes and scores per your record-retention policy. At least 1 year from the hiring decision is the federal baseline under 29 CFR 1602.14; some states add longer retention for AI-assisted decisions (for example, California's ADS regulations keep automated-decision records 4 years, per the law reference). Check the job state's row, and confirm with counsel.

## 6. Time budget per question
- Behavioral or situational: about 3–4 minutes
- Technical: about 1–2 minutes
- Practical test: as specified in the test file

A 45-minute interview holds about 10–12 questions plus the opening and the candidate's own questions.
